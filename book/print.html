Purpose & Profit

About

These are study notes on Dan Koe, Purpose & Profit, Essays Volume 1 (KOE Press, 2025, ISBN 978-1-936961-28-3).

The book is his. These pages paraphrase the argument and keep short quotes so a note can be checked against a page. They do not reproduce the book.

He asks readers who want the book to buy the paperback or the Kindle edition.

The Big Picture

Study notes on Purpose & Profit (Essays Volume 1) by Dan Koe. Twelve essays, sixty-six sections. Each section is a paraphrase, a few short quotes checked against the book, and questions. This site is not the book.

Start with the cheat sheet. Concept names are on the concepts page. Everyday words are on the themes page.

Koe argues that schools and jobs condition people to build other people's dreams, and that the answer is to merge purpose and profit rather than reject money. Agency, generalism, and solving your own problems move you through four levels of purpose: survival, status, creativity, and contribution. The practical path is a plan (anti-vision, vision, goals, projects, levers), persuasion as ethical power, writing as the meta-skill, and becoming the niche by selling the solution to your own problems. That makes you a creator in what he calls a Second Renaissance.

Cheat Sheet

The big picture

The core idea

Summary (paraphrase): Koe argues that school and jobs train people to build someone else's dreams, and that the way out is to merge purpose and profit instead of rejecting money. You grow by choosing and solving problems, starting with your own. A written plan, writing as a core skill, and a product built around your own development turn that into a self-directed life.

Main points

1. You were trained to build other people's dreams

Paraphrase: School and work quietly teach you to serve someone else's plan.

"While they taught us our ABCs, they silently encoded a deeper lesson: Your purpose is to build their dreams, not your own."

3. Conditioned to Work for Others

2. Entrepreneurship means solving problems

Paraphrase: The point is control over your earnings, lifestyle, and suffering, gained by solving problems.

"The entire purpose of entrepreneurship is to have full control over your earnings, lifestyle, and suffering by creating solutions to problems."

13. Solve the Problem

3. Problems are the path

Paraphrase: Each problem you solve makes life feel easier and raises your sense of purpose.

"With each problem you solve, the perceived difficulty of life goes down, and your level of purpose goes up."

28. Problems Are the Path

4. Purpose grows in stages

Paraphrase: Survival, status, creativity, and contribution are stages to move through, not ranks.

"With this philosophy, none of these levels are bad or “less than” another."

29. The Four Levels and 1. Survival

5. Lost, interested, obsessed

Paraphrase: Try many things until one grabs you, then go deep.

"The secret is to try everything until you find that one thing that you can’t pull yourself away from. When you find that one thing, go deep."

42. Experiment Inward, Outward, Downward, Upward

6. A plan is your surface area for luck

Paraphrase: Having a plan makes good outcomes more likely, and you already follow one whether you admit it or not.

"A plan is your surface area for luck, and if you don’t have one, or think you don’t need one, you may not realize that you are already acting toward one."

44. A Plan Is Your Surface Area for Luck

7. You are the niche

Paraphrase: Your niche is not something out there to find. It grows as you develop and solve your own problems.

"Your niche isn’t a static target—it’s a living, breathing extension of your personal development."

59. Create a Product, and the Trouble With Choosing a Niche

Say it in one minute

Paraphrase: Most of us were raised to be useful workers on someone else's plan. Koe says the fix is not to hate money but to merge purpose and profit by solving problems, first your own. Purpose grows through stages: survival, status, creativity, contribution. To find your direction, experiment widely until something hooks you, then go deep. Write a plan with an anti-vision, a vision, and projects, learn by building, start writing, and sell the solutions to problems you have already solved. You are the niche.

"You think making money has a luck factor when luck is a concept used to describe a lack of understanding of a system."

"Your anti-vision is the future that you do not want to live."

3. What does a mission do?

"Your mission is the most important thing in your life. It is the bridge between what you do and don’t want."

48. Mission, Standards and Goals

"Learning comes from struggle, not memorization."

49. Projects, Constraints and Levers

5. Why write?

  • Rejecting money is not the answer. The only option is to merge purpose and profit.
