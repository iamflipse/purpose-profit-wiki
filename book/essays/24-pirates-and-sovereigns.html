24. Pirates, Slaves and Sovereign Individuals

The big picture | Cheat sheet | Concepts | Themes

Essay: Deep Generalism
Book pages: pp. 40 to 41

Summary

Koe argues schools promised the prestige of specialization to keep bright minds narrow. He contrasts the Royal Historian with the pirates, who knew navigation, crews, economics and science. Roman slaves did one task for life, while sovereign individuals did many things. A good test: your work should change at least once a year.

Key ideas

  • Schools promised specialization's prestige to keep bright minds narrow.
  • Pirates (high-agency entrepreneurs) knew many domains needed for trade.
  • Rulers were only as powerful as the resources pirates allowed them.
  • Roman slaves did one task for life; sovereign individuals did many things.
  • Your work should change at least every year as you and your interests evolve.

Quotes

"Schools were created to enslave the brightest minds by promising the prestige of specialization so they remained narrow minded and didn’t overthrow the true rulers."

(p. 40, about word 7277; concepts: Specialization vs Generalism, Interest-Based Education, Conditioning and Programming)

"the pirates—or high-agency entrepreneurs of the past—understood many things like geography, celestial navigation, the crew on their ship, the ship itself, economics, history, and science"

(p. 40, about word 7325; concepts: Deep Generalism, Skill Stacking, Agency)

"Even further, if we look at Roman civilization, slaves were trained to do one specific task for the entirety of their lives."

(p. 40, about word 7388; concepts: Specialization vs Generalism, Freedom and Sovereignty)

"The sovereign individuals, on the other hand, were expected to act on their own interest and do many things throughout their lives."

(p. 40, about word 7416; concepts: Freedom and Sovereignty, Deep Generalism)

"A good metric for determining if you are on the right path is if your work changes at a minimum of every year."

(p. 40, about word 7456; concepts: Learning, Persistence and Iteration)

(p. 41, about word 7497; concepts: AI and the Future of Work, Freedom and Sovereignty)

Questions to explore

  • Are you a historian or a pirate in your field?
  • How much has your work changed in the past year?
  • What one task are you at risk of doing forever?
