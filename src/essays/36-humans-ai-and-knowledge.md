# 36. Humans, AI and the Two Functions of Knowledge

[The big picture](../index.md) | [Cheat sheet](../cheatsheet.md) | [Concepts](../concepts.md) | [Themes](../themes.md)

**Essay:** Progress and Knowledge  
**Book pages:** pp. 67 to 68  

## Summary
Koe argues AI doesn't make humans irrelevant: there's no limit to what we can compute or transform with the right knowledge. Humans may have an edge in attention, because we can change perspective like lenses on a camera. Knowledge makes specific things happen and captures patterns in reality. The way to create the knowledge you need lies in the unknown, through new goals that lead into uncertainty.

## Key ideas
- AI may seem to render humans irrelevant; Koe says this is not the case.
- No limit to what humans can compute or transform with the right knowledge.
- Humans may have an edge in attention: changing focus by changing perspective.
- Perspective, backed by consciousness and experience, is something AI may never grasp.
- Knowledge makes specific things happen and captures patterns in reality.
- New knowledge comes from setting a new goal, especially one leading to uncertainty.
