# 34. Search Problems and Cybernetics

[The big picture](../index.md) | [Cheat sheet](../cheatsheet.md) | [Concepts](../concepts.md) | [Themes](../themes.md)

**Essay:** Progress and Knowledge  
**Book pages:** pp. 63 to 64  

## Summary
You aren't where you want to be because you lack the knowledge. Koe borrows David Deutsch's framing of life as a search problem in a vast space of ideas. He then turns to AI and its origin in Norbert Wiener's cybernetics: systems that act, sense and compare against a goal, like a tightrope walker or a ship steering by a lighthouse. When feedback stops, the system falls into chaos.

## Key ideas
- You lack results because you lack the knowledge to get them.
- A better life is a search problem in an idea space too large to search exhaustively.
- How do we explore the unknown in the right direction?
- AI grew out of Wiener's cybernetics (1948): self-regulating control.
- Acting, sensing and comparing to a goal is the fundamental loop of intelligent systems.
- Tightrope walkers and ships near lighthouses stay stable through constant small corrections.
- When feedback stops, the system falls into chaos.
