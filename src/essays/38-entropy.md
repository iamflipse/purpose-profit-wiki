# 38. Entropy: The Enemy of Progress

[The big picture](../index.md) | [Cheat sheet](../cheatsheet.md) | [Concepts](../concepts.md) | [Themes](../themes.md)

**Essay:** Progress and Knowledge  
**Book pages:** pp. 70 to 71  

## Summary
There is no final destination, only better problems. If you aren't wrestling with a meaningful problem, you aren't producing value. Entropy, the tendency of all systems to fall into chaos unless effort keeps them ordered, is the enemy of progress, like a dirty room. Humans survive conceptually as well as physically, protecting identity through concepts.

## Key ideas
- There will only ever be better problems; no final destination.
- Not wrestling with a meaningful problem means not producing value; you lose purpose.
- Entropy: all systems fall into chaos unless effort maintains order.
- A dirty room degrades slowly, then all at once.
- Humans survive on the conceptual level, reproducing information in their consciousness.
- We protect identity through concepts: books, arguments, knowledge.
