# 45. You Know What You Want

[The big picture](../index.md) | [Cheat sheet](../cheatsheet.md) | [Concepts](../concepts.md) | [Themes](../themes.md)

**Essay:** Your Life’s Work  
**Book pages:** pp. 82 to 83  

## Summary
Saying 'I don't know what I want' usually means 'I don't want to do the work.' Knowing what you don't want tells you what you want, and people hide from the pain of reinventing their identity. Koe lists what he didn't want: a job he hated, work he didn't care about, a low-energy body, an arguing partner and a mind he couldn't grip. Those told him what to do.

## Key ideas
- 'I don't know what I want' usually means not wanting to do the work.
- Knowing what you don't want means you know what you want.
- People hide from the slow structural redesign of identity.
- Observe society to see what you don't want.
- If you don't know where to start, start with those conditioned problems.
- Koe's plan: become an entrepreneur, gain power over unwanted work, train daily, prioritize energy.
