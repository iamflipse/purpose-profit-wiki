# 26. Kosmos, Holons and the Circle of Concern

[The big picture](../index.md) | [Cheat sheet](../cheatsheet.md) | [Concepts](../concepts.md) | [Themes](../themes.md)

**Essay:** Levels of Purpose  
**Book pages:** pp. 45 to 47  

## Summary
The essay opens with the question 'Who should I become?' Koe draws on the Pythagorean Kosmos, an ordered whole, and Koestler's holon, a whole that is also part of something larger. Development moves from egocentric to Kosmocentric, widening your circle of care. Fulfillment comes from caring for others, so money isn't the only profit, and lower levels are integrated, not abandoned.

## Key ideas
- What you do must start with who you should become.
- Kosmos is an ordered, harmonious whole, unlike a merely physical universe of parts.
- A holon is a whole that is also a part; reality forms a natural holarchy.
- Development runs egocentric to ethnocentric to worldcentric to planetcentric to Kosmocentric.
- Fulfillment comes from caring for others through progressive overload of responsibility.
- Money isn't the only form of profit.
- You integrate lower levels rather than break free of them.
