# 23. Prussian Schools and Education as Discovery

[The big picture](../index.md) | [Cheat sheet](../cheatsheet.md) | [Concepts](../concepts.md) | [Themes](../themes.md)

**Essay:** Deep Generalism  
**Book pages:** pp. 37 to 40  

## Summary
Koe traces American schooling to Horace Mann's import of the Prussian system, which was designed to produce obedient soldiers and workers and taught people how to work rather than how to think. Standardization holds back the gifted and traps the rest. A narrow goal turns you into a niche specialist, like a lion that can't survive in Alaska. Education should be discovery, with interest as the lighthouse.

## Key ideas
- The Prussian system was designed for obedient soldiers, compliant citizens and workers.
- Students were taught how to work, not how to think.
- You didn't create or fund the system, so it doesn't serve you.
- Standardization slows the gifted and leaves no creative room for the rest.
- You learn only as much as your top-of-mind goal allows.
- Niche specialists fail when swapped, like a lion and a polar bear.
- Education is discovery: interest as lighthouse, collecting and connecting dots, sharing them.
